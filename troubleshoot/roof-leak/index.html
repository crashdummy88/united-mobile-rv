Troubleshoot

Roof leak / soft deck

Find the entry path before you coat the whole roof. Soft decks mean water has already moved inside the laminate — map first, coat second.

Walk seams, screw heads, vent flanges, and antenna mounts in daylight. Lap sealant that is cracked, chalked, or pulled away is a classic path. Interior stains often show up downhill from the real hole — a wet corner over the bed can start at a front cap joint or an AC shroud two bays forward. Do not buy five gallons of coating until you know where water is getting in.

Quick path

  1. Map interior stains to the nearest roof penetration uphill. Mark the ceiling with tape and take photos.
  2. Inspect lap sealant, self-leveling caulk, and membrane seams. Look for alligatoring, voids, and lifted edges.
  3. Press-test soft spots carefully with a flat hand — don’t punch through a wet deck. Note spongy feel vs solid.
  4. Check AC shroud screws, gasket condition, vent flanges, ladder mounts, and antenna / dish plates.
  5. Document with photos before a mobile tech arrives — wet vs dry day photos both help.

Common forks

  • Stain after rain, dry roof walk — slow seam leak or wind-driven rain at a flange; re-check after the next storm.
  • Soft deck near a vent — flange seal failure; stop walking that area until inspected.
  • Leak only while driving — often front cap / radius joints or a poorly sealed aftermarket mount.
  • Musty smell, no visible stain yet — early intrusion; thermal moisture mapping beats ripping headliner blind.

Don’t skip

  • Front/rear cap joints and ladder mounts.
  • AC shroud screws and gasket condition — retorqued shrouds without resealing are a classic DIY miss.
  • Satellite / Starlink / antenna plates — seal integrity after any roof work. Clear-sky mounts still need a weatherproof boot.
  • Skylight and MaxxFan flanges; bathroom vent caulk lines.

When to stop DIY

  • Soft deck you can flex with light pressure — structural risk; Prefer Text.
  • You are about to coat over dirt, mildew, or an unsound membrane.
  • Active drip onto electrical or into a wall cavity with unknown extent.
  • Working at height in wind without fall awareness — wait for a mobile tech with the right setup.
