Roof leak / soft deck

Find the entry path before you coat the whole roof. Soft decks mean water has already moved inside the laminate — map first, coat second.

Map before you coat. Walk seams, screw heads, vent flanges, and antenna mounts in daylight. Lap sealant that is cracked, chalked, or pulled away is a classic path. Interior stains often show up downhill from the real hole — a wet corner over the bed can start at a front cap joint or an AC shroud two bays forward. EPDM and TPO membranes fail differently than sealant alone; coating over dirt, mildew, or an unsound deck locks moisture in. Do not buy five gallons of coating until you know where water is getting in.

Symptoms

  • Ceiling stain that grows after rain (or only after wind-driven rain)
  • Musty smell in a closet or over a slide with no obvious drip yet
  • Soft or spongy deck near a vent, AC, skylight, or ladder mount
  • Lap sealant cracked, alligatoring, or lifted at screw heads
  • Leak only while driving / only at highway speeds
  • AC condensate vs true roof intrusion confusion (wet near the AC chase)
  • Aftermarket dish / antenna / Starlink plate with a tired boot or open screw ring

Quick path (owner-safe)

  1. Map interior stains to the nearest roof penetration uphill. Mark the ceiling with tape and take photos on a wet day and a dry day.
  2. Inspect lap sealant, self-leveling caulk, and membrane seams. Look for alligatoring, voids, and lifted edges — especially on EPDM lap lines and around older butyl.
  3. Press-test soft spots carefully with a flat hand — don’t punch through a wet deck. Note spongy feel vs solid.
  4. Check AC shroud screws, gasket condition, vent flanges (bathroom, range, fridge), ladder mounts, and antenna / dish plates.
  5. Differentiate AC condensate drain paths from roof entry — a wet chase under the AC is not always a membrane hole.
  6. Document with photos before a mobile tech arrives; note recent roof work, coating, or wireless mounts.

Symptom forks

  • Stain after rain, dry roof walk — slow seam leak or wind-driven rain at a flange; re-check after the next storm with chalk/tape markers.
  • Soft deck near a vent — flange seal failure; stop walking that area until inspected.
  • Leak only while driving — often front cap / radius joints or a poorly sealed aftermarket mount seeing wind pressure.
  • Musty smell, no visible stain yet — early intrusion; thermal moisture mapping beats ripping headliner blind.
  • Wet only under the AC — condensate management vs gasket failure; don’t coat the whole roof for a drain hose.
  • New wireless mount, old stain pattern — re-inspect the boot and screw ring; clear-sky mounts still need a weatherproof seal (Starlink install capability — not a “Starlink Certified” claim).

Don’t skip (sealants, vents, AC gasket, EPDM)

  • Front/rear cap joints and ladder mounts — high-motion, high-miss areas.
  • AC shroud screws and gasket condition — retorqued shrouds without resealing are a classic DIY miss.
  • Self-leveling vs non-leveling sealant used in the wrong place (vertical wall vs flat roof puddle).
  • EPDM chalking, open laps, and failed tape repairs under a cosmetic coating.
  • Skylight and MaxxFan flanges; bathroom vent caulk lines; fridge roof jack.
  • Satellite / Starlink / antenna plates — seal integrity after any roof work.

Owner checklist

  • Roof membrane type if known (EPDM / TPO / fiberglass) and any prior coating brand/year.
  • Photos of stains inside, soft spots, and every penetration you can safely reach.
  • AC brand/model and whether the shroud was recently removed.
  • Recent aftermarket mounts (dish, antenna, Starlink, weBoost) and who sealed them.
  • Coach year/make/model and parking location for a trip quote.

When to stop DIY

  • Soft deck you can flex with light pressure — structural risk; Prefer Text.
  • You are about to coat over dirt, mildew, or an unsound membrane.
  • Active drip onto electrical, into a wall cavity, or onto 12V/120V gear — stop and dry-isolate; Prefer Text.
  • Working at height in wind without fall awareness — wait for a mobile tech with the right setup.
  • Delaminated walls, swollen cabinetry, or ceiling panels that sag — beyond a tube of Dicor.

What UMRT does on a roof call
