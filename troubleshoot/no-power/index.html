Troubleshoot

No power / dead coach

Work the symptom from shore inlet → breakers → converter → house batteries before guessing parts. A dark coach is usually a path problem, not a whole-coach rebuild.

Start safe: confirm the pedestal breaker and your shore cord ends before opening panels. Do not stand in wet ground while resetting a GFI pedestal, and do not bypass a failed EMS by jerry-rigging bare copper. A dark coach is often an open breaker, a failed surge protector/EMS, a converter that quit, or a battery disconnect that got bumped — not “the inverter is dead forever.”

Quick path

  1. Verify pedestal power; try a known-good outlet or different pedestal when possible. Confirm the shore cord is fully seated at both ends.
  2. Check coach main breakers and the EMS / surge protector status lights. Note any fault LEDs before you reset anything twice.
  3. Listen/feel for the converter fan; measure converter output and battery voltage with a DVOM if you have one. Write the numbers down.
  4. Separate 120V symptoms from 12V drain (lights, slides, furnace board, water pump). If 12V is alive and 120V is not, stay on the shore path.
  5. Check the battery disconnect switch and any main house fuse / ANL / MIDI fuse before you condemn the bank.

Common forks

  • No 120V only — shore/EMS/breaker path; house 12V may still be fine. Do not start by replacing the inverter.
  • No 12V only — battery, disconnect, converter/charger, or a main fuse. Lights and slides go dark while the microwave might still work on shore.
  • Both dark — start at the pedestal and work inward. Confirm the coach is not on “storage mode” or a parasitic drain that killed the bank overnight.
  • Intermittent / brownout — loose shore pin, weak pedestal, or converter thermal foldback under AC + microwave load. Note when it fails.

Owner checklist (photos help)

  • Pedestal breaker amp rating vs your cord (30A vs 50A adapters matter).
  • EMS / surge protector model and any fault code on the display.
  • Converter / charger brand and model sticker; Victron users — note MultiPlus / Cerbo fault text.
  • Battery type (flooded / AGM / Li) and resting voltage if you can measure it.
  • Year / make / model of the coach and where you are parked.

When to stop DIY

  • Burning smell, scorched shore plug, or melted adapter — unplug and Prefer Text.
  • You need to open sealed EMS housings or cut factory harnesses to “test.”
  • Lithium BMS lockout, inverter AC-out faults you cannot clear, or neutral-ground bonding questions on a bonded pedestal.
  • Any work that requires thermal imaging of hot breakers or torque on lug connections you are not equipped for.
