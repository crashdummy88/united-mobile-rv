Troubleshoot

weBoost weak signal

A cellular booster amplifies usable outdoor signal into the coach. It will not invent RF from zero, and “more gain” is not a knob we turn — weBoost uses AGC. If the outside world is a dead zone, the honest fix is a different path (Starlink, a better campground spot, or accepting physics) — not a bigger box. Prefer Text for Authorized Installer diagnostics.

Symptoms

  • Interior bars barely move after install; calls still drop
  • Booster LED / app shows oscillation, overload, or error states
  • Works near the window, fails deeper in the coach
  • Worked parked, fails after travel (antenna shift, coax strain, ground loss)
  • Hot booster or power supply; intermittent reboot
  • Peplink cellular WAN still flaps even with booster “on”
  • Owner bought a kit expecting “always 5 bars” in canyons / heavy timber

Quick checks (owner-safe)

  1. Outside first: with the booster off, check whether your phone has any usable outdoor signal where the exterior antenna sits. No outdoor signal → booster cannot invent coverage.
  2. LED / app status: note exact lights or app messages (oscillation / overload / soft install). Photo them.
  3. Separation: confirm exterior and interior antennas have enough physical separation / isolation per OEM — oscillation is often an install geometry problem, not a “bad amp.”
  4. Coax & connectors: check for kinked coax, water in connectors, loose N/SMA fittings after travel days.
  5. Power: fused DC, correct voltage, no shared circuit that sags under inverter load.
  6. Carrier reality: verify SIM / plan is active; bars ≠ throughput; try a speed test outdoors vs indoors with booster on/off.
  7. Disable experiments: temporary VPNs / Wi-Fi calling quirks can mask whether the booster path actually improved RF.

When to stop DIY

  • Roof / ladder-rack mounts with seal failure or spinning hardware
  • Water in coax connectors or corroded fittings you cannot replace correctly
  • Hot / melting power supplies or scorched wiring
  • Cutting proprietary antenna cables without OEM guidance
  • Any plan to “crank gain” or flash non-OEM firmware
  • Working on a wet roof or near power lines

What UMRT diagnoses

  • Outdoor signal survey at the proposed exterior antenna location (honesty first)
  • Antenna isolation / oscillation vs true weak-signal path
  • Coax quality, grounding, and fused DC integration (install quality vs zip-tie ladder specials)
  • Interaction with Peplink WAN Health Check / failover when both are present
  • Whether Starlink / Peplink multi-WAN is the better architecture for your travel pattern (weBoost Authorized Installer · Peplink Certified Associate · Starlink install capability)

DVOM on the DC feed when brownout is suspected; we do not invent RF meters-as-marketing.

Outdoor signal required (honesty)

If the exterior antenna sits in a null (deep canyon, dense timber, metal carport), the booster has nothing useful to amplify. Moving the coach ten feet, raising the antenna, or accepting Starlink for that stretch is often smarter than buying a second kit.

AGC — we don’t “tune gain”

weBoost systems use automatic gain control. We place antennas, set isolation, and verify LEDs/app health — we do not promise manual gain tricks or guaranteed bar counts. Marketing screenshots are not a field test.

Oscillation vs weak signal

  • Oscillation — interior and exterior antennas hearing each other; fix geometry / isolation / barriers.
  • Overload — too much outdoor signal near a tower; OEM guidance on attenuation / placement.
  • True weak signal — outdoor is marginal; booster helps some, physics still wins in the hole.

Power and travel damage

Travel days loosen mounts and chew coax. A kit that passed at install can fail after corrugated road. Re-check connectors and antenna aim before condemning the amplifier. Shared flaky 12V feeds cause “booster randomly dies” tickets that are really power tickets — see /troubleshoot/12v-battery-no-charge/.

How this fits Starlink + Peplink

Many coaches run weBoost → Peplink cellular WAN alongside Starlink. Diagnose each path separately, then look at WAN priority. Combined road-dropout guide: /troubleshoot/starlink-peplink-dropouts/.

Service installs: /wireless/.

Install quality vs ladder-rack specials

A booster bolted to a ladder with exposed coax is a different product than one grounded, fused, and isolated into the coach. When diagnosing “this kit never worked,” separate:

  • Kit limitation — outdoor null / wrong band expectations
  • Install geometry — oscillation from interior/exterior too close
  • Travel damage — connectors, water, mounts
  • Power path — sag, shared circuits, undersized feed

UMRT installs treat wireless like electrical work: mount, seal, fuse, integrate. See /wireless/.

LED / app triage (owner capture)

Before Prefer Text, capture:

  1. Exact LED color/pattern or app status string
  2. Whether the fault changes with doors/windows open (isolation clue)
  3. Outdoor phone test at the exterior antenna location (booster off)
  4. Indoor test at the interior antenna with booster on vs off (same spot, same carrier)

Bring those four data points — it cuts diagnostic time.

Campground and canyon expectations

Evening campground congestion and deep timber / canyon walls will beat any consumer booster. We will say so up front. Multi-WAN (Peplink + Starlink) is often the architecture for travelers who need resilience — not a third weBoost kit stacked on hope.
