LP gas systems

Manometer pressure tests, combustible gas leak detection, regulators, and appliance feed verification — propane safety done with the right tools.

Active corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

How the system works

Propane leaves the tank as liquid and drops through a two-stage regulator to working pressure for furnace, water heater, fridge, cooktop, and often the generator. A failing regulator hits every appliance at once — and often gets misdiagnosed as three separate appliance failures.

Services

LP inspection & repair

Regulator testing & replacement

Pressure at regulator outlet and at appliances — find drop across the supply and confirm correct working pressure.

Leak detection

Every connection checked with a calibrated combustible gas leak detector — tank, regulator, lines, valves, appliances.

Supply line inspection

Rubber-coated copper and flexible stainless age, crack at fittings, and take vibration/rodent damage.

Appliance feed verification

Correct pressure and flow at each appliance — not just at the regulator.

Shutoff & detector checks

Manual shutoffs operable; propane/CO detector function tested. Detectors age out — usually replace by ~5 years.

Regulator vent & cover

Blocked vents and restrictive covers are common causes of regulator malfunction.

Owner safety

Propane is heavier than air — it sinks into floors and compartments. Know your tank shutoff. Weak yellow flames, sudden short-cycling furnaces, or a fridge that won't stay lit on gas are system symptoms. Inspect after storage before you light everything up.
