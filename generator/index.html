Service coverage

What we diagnose and fix

No-start diagnosis

Fuel delivery, ignition, choke, starter, and control-board signals — carb fouling and fuel degradation after storage are common.

Surging & unstable output

Carburetor / governor issues that hunt RPM or produce unstable voltage under load.

Overload & shutdown

Low output, failing AVR, brush wear, or circuit overload when A/C comes online.

Oil & fuel service

Oil, air filter, plugs, fuel filter — ethanol-blended fuel left sitting is a primary failure driver.

Brushes, AVR, fault codes

Wear items in the generator head plus Onan fault-code interpretation in context.

Exhaust & mounting

Exhaust leaks are a CO hazard; connections, muffler, and compartment sealing checked on service visits.

FAQ

Generator service questions we get a lot

How much does RV generator repair cost?

Depends entirely on what's wrong — a fuel system service and annual maintenance run far less than a carbon-brush or AVR replacement. We diagnose on-site before quoting any repair. See pricing for base rates.

Why won't my RV generator start?

Most common: stale or ethanol-degraded fuel gumming up the carburetor, a stuck choke, a failed fuel pump, or a control-board fault code you can't see without a proper diagnostic read. Generators that sat all winter without a fuel treatment are the most common no-start call every spring.

Why does my generator shut down when I turn on the AC?

Classic overload-under-load symptom — usually low output voltage, a failing AVR, worn carbon brushes, or a genuinely overloaded circuit. We load-test output voltage and frequency to isolate engine-side vs. generator-head faults before recommending a repair.

Do you work on Onan, Generac, and Kohler generators?

Yes — Onan is the most common brand we service, followed by Generac and Kohler. Also NPS-certified for the full NPS line, plus portables from Honda, Champion, and Westinghouse.

Do I need a full service if it's been sitting all winter?

A post-storage inspection and fuel system service is worth doing before the first trip of the season — it catches the fuel-gumming and hard-starting issues that develop from sitting, before they strand you at a campsite with no shore power.
