Battery chemistry: why it changes everything

The single biggest mistake in RV battery systems is treating lithium (LiFePO4), AGM, and flooded lead-acid as interchangeable. They need different charge voltages, discharge habits, and storage practices — a charger set for one chemistry will damage another over time.

Lithium (LiFePO4)

Deep-cycle friendly with a working BMS. Does not want lead-acid float habits. Cold charge inhibit near freezing is common — confirm your battery/BMS manual (many inhibit around 0 °C / 32 °F; heated packs differ). Do not invent a universal cutoff.

AGM

Sealed, no watering, but repeated discharge below about 50% shortens life. Needs correct absorb/float — not a lithium profile bolted on.

Flooded lead-acid

Needs watering and occasional equalization per manufacturer. Most abuse-tolerant to casual DIY — still easy to sulfate in storage.

Mixed banks

Paralleling unlike chemistries or ages is how mystery drains and charger fights start. Fix topology before buying another bigger battery.

Battery not holding a charge

  • Lithium: BMS protection from deep discharge, over-temperature, or cell imbalance is far more common than a truly dead pack
  • Lead-acid / AGM: sulfation from repeated deep discharge or long storage without a maintainer
  • Parasitic draw: something left on, inverter standby, or aftermarket always-hot loads
  • Charger / converter: wrong profile, failed stage sensing, or open internal fuse
  • Cable & lugs: voltage looks fine at rest at the battery and collapses at the inverter under load

Capacity testing: is the battery actually bad?

Resting voltage alone does not tell you much — a battery can read a normal resting voltage and still fail to deliver capacity under load. A proper capacity test (controlled discharge with a known load, or manufacturer-approved tester workflow) is the only way to know. Replace on data, not on “it looks old.”

Storage & maintenance charging

Lithium storage

Store around 50–60% SoC — not 100% for months. Confirm cold storage rules in your BMS manual.

Lead-acid / AGM storage

Store fully charged on a maintenance/float charger sized for the bank. A “full” battery left alone all winter often returns sulfated.

Solar not charging or underperforming

Shading & array

Partial shading from a vent, A/C, or antenna can drop output far more than the shaded area suggests — especially on non-optimized strings.

Controller profile

Wrong battery type, wrong system voltage, or remote/BMS control disabling charge. Confirm settings in the app or display before condemning panels.

Wiring & fusing

High resistance MC4s, undersized cable, open fuses, and loose lugs impersonate “bad solar.”

Cold Voc risk

Cold mornings raise panel Voc — string design must stay under the MPPT max. Overvoltage errors are often design, not a failed controller.

Inverter faults & shutdowns

Low-voltage shutdown

Bank sagged under load — undersized cabling, high resistance lugs, BMS load disconnect, or a bank smaller than the surge.

Overload shutdown

Connected load or inrush exceeded capability — microwave + induction + compressor stories are classic.

Overtemp

Hot bay, blocked airflow, failed fan. Derate expectations for summer mountain climbs with continuous loads.

Ripple / connection quality

Loose DC lugs beat board replacement more often than owners want to hear. Meter at the inverter under load.

FAQ

Solar is zero on a sunny day?

Confirm array Voc/Vmp at the controller, breaker/fuse state, and whether BMS/GX remote control disabled charging. Cold-charge inhibit looks like “MPPT broken.”

Can I charge lithium with my stock converter?

Only if the profile matches the battery requirements. Many stock converters are lead-acid-centric and will mistreat LiFePO4.

Do I need a bigger inverter?

Maybe — or you need honest cable, surge math, and bank capacity first. Peak stickers are not continuous ratings.

Where do Victron manuals live?

Official Victron live wiki and manuals hub only. Software twin: umrt-software VictronConnect troubleshoot.
