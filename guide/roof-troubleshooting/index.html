What to Check and How Often

— Inspect all roof seams, vents, skylights, and antenna mounts at least twice a year — Check around every roof penetration — anything through the roof is a potential entry point — Look at sidewall seams at the rooflin.

Identify Your Roof Type Before You Touch It

Using the wrong sealant or coating on your roof type doesn’t just fail to help — it can actively damage the membrane. Know what you have before you buy anything.

DIY Roof Leak Water Test

Takes about 20 minutes and tells you whether you actually have a leak, and roughly where it is, before you call anyone.

Signs You Already Have a Problem

— A ceiling stain, even a faint one — the source has usually been active for months already — Soft or spongy flooring, especially near slide-outs or exterior walls — A musty smell that doesn’t go away with airing out.

Common Mistake: Wrong Sealant

Don’t use silicone on an RV roof unless you know exactly what the roofing material is.

Owner checklist
  • Pick a dry day and have a helper inside with a flashlight, checking the ceiling around every vent, skylight, antenna mount, and seam
  • Starting at the lowest point of the roof, run a garden hose (no nozzle, gentle flow, not a pressure washer) over one section at a time
  • Hold each section for 3–5 minutes before moving to the next — slow soaks find leaks that a quick pass misses
  • Work systematically front to back, one penetration or seam at a time, so you know exactly which section caused any water your helper sees
  • Press gently on the ceiling near vents and lights afterward — a soft or spongy spot means moisture has already reached the substrate even without a visible drip
